Tile tracking is where good players become expert players.

## The practical threshold

High-level players often begin serious tracking once they have reduced uncertainty to roughly 20 unseen tiles.

At this point, quality of inference improves because:

- rack possibilities narrow,
- probability curves stabilize,
- blank and high-value tile impact becomes visible.

## Why blanks and Q matter so much

If a blank still might be live, many plays become better than their pure tile score.

If Q is still out, pressure on hooks and open consonants changes immediately.

That is why advanced players use move trees based on probable unseen composition, not just present rack instinct.

## Tracking method you can use tonight

1. Keep a quick ledger of all played tiles by letter frequency.
2. Mark probable exchanges and known exchanges near endgame.
3. Re-rank candidate moves when the unseen pool shifts.

When possible, use [Word Unscrambler](https://monkeytactics.com/tools/word-unscrambler) for verification and to compare legal options under each probability branch.

## Avoiding common mistakes

- treating every turn as independent,
- refreshing from memory instead of evidence,
- or ignoring board timing when probabilities improve.

Tile tracking is hard, but consistent evidence-based tracking usually beats intuition in close games.

## What "about 20 tiles" means in practical play

Early in the game, tile uncertainty is too high to make strong inferences.

Around the 20-unknown mark:

- legal possibilities narrow,
- opponent pressure can be modeled more clearly,
- and small probability changes become actionable.

This is not a strict threshold; it is a working model.

## Practical tracking ledger format

Use three columns for each move:

- `Seen` (board placements and exchanges),
- `Possible` (what the opponent likely has),
- `Pressure` (how much your rack/board plan depends on those possibilities).

Keep the table minimal and update every two turns.

## Bayesian-style intuition for Scrabble

Without heavy formulas, think in weighted branches:

- if a blank is still possible, raise your willingness for longer risk.
- if Q is still off-board and unopened, increase hook urgency.
- if both happen, prioritize flexible board-access over pure score.

This is strategy informed by probability, not fear.

## Common tile-tracking errors

1. Tracking only vowels and ignoring consonant clusters.
2. Forgetting exchange impact on revealed possibilities.
3. Treating every missed guess as luck instead of data.

Each of these is fixable with the same simple ritual: log before acting.

## 14-minute tile tracking protocol

Use this protocol once per game after move 3:

- 4 minutes: clean ledger reset.
- 4 minutes: probability branch refresh.
- 4 minutes: candidate re-filter through leave and hook filters.
- 2 minutes: move validation.

Repeat until game end, or until opponent tempo dominates.
