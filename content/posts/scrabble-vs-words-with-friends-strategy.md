Scrabble and Words With Friends look like close relatives. Both give you seven tiles, ask you to build connected words on a 15-by-15 grid, and reward clever use of letter and word multipliers. That similarity makes one mistake especially easy: using the same scoring instincts, word list, and solver for both games.

The strongest move can change when you switch games. A word may have a different base score. A premium square may sit in a different place. A seven-tile play earns a different bonus. Even a word accepted in one game may be rejected in the other.

| Rack size | 7 tiles | 7 tiles | Rack balance and leave quality matter in both. |

## The same letters do not have the same value

Several common letters are worth more in Words With Friends than in Scrabble. That changes both the apparent value of a rack and the order in which candidate words should be considered.

| Letter | Scrabble | Words With Friends |
|---|---:|---:|
| B | 3 | 4 |
| C | 3 | 4 |
| G | 2 | 3 |
| H | 4 | 3 |
| J | 8 | 10 |
| L | 1 | 2 |
| M | 3 | 4 |
| N | 1 | 2 |
| U | 1 | 2 |
| V | 4 | 5 |
| Y | 4 | 3 |

The tables agree on some headline tiles—`Q` and `Z` are worth 10, while `X` is worth 8—but enough values differ to break a generic “highest-scoring word” calculation.

Consider `JAMB`. Its base value is 14 in Scrabble: `J` 8, `A` 1, `M` 3, and `B` 3. In Words With Friends it is worth 19: `J` 10, `A` 1, `M` 4, and `B` 4. Before any board multiplier, the WWF version is already five points richer.

This is the most concrete difference between the two MonkeyTactics tools. Search the same rack in both and the candidate words may be identical, because you chose the same source dictionary, but their scores and score-first ranking can differ. Use the game-specific solver before comparing moves.

## Board geometry changes the best move

Both boards use double-letter, triple-letter, double-word, and triple-word squares. The multipliers work in the familiar way, but their locations are different.

Scrabble's premium pattern encourages careful control of the star-to-corner lanes and makes opening a triple-word line a major defensive decision. Words With Friends distributes its premiums differently, including tempting letter and word multipliers near the board's edges. The result is a game in which short words, parallel plays, and high-value letters placed on a `DL` or `TL` square frequently compete with longer words.

That changes the question you ask on every turn:

- In Scrabble, ask whether the move opens a dangerous double- or triple-word lane and whether the leave justifies that risk.
- In Words With Friends, inspect every compact lane that lets one tile score in multiple crossing words, especially when that tile can reach a letter multiplier.
- In both games, remember that a premium counts only when a newly placed tile covers it. A result's base score is never the full board score.

The official Words With Friends site emphasizes connecting new words while maximizing `TW` and `TL` squares, and Zynga's rules confirm that all words formed in a play are scored. [Words With Friends Classic overview](https://wordswithfriends.com/) and [Zynga rule book](https://zyngasupport.helpshift.com/hc/en/63-words-with-friends-2/faq/10552-words-with-friends-rule-book/?contact=1).

## Bingo strategy: 50 points changes the math

Playing all seven rack tiles is powerful in both games, but Scrabble's 50-point bonus is 15 points larger than Words With Friends' 35-point bonus.

That gap affects leave decisions. Suppose one move scores 28 but destroys a flexible `AEINST` core, while another scores 21 and preserves it. In Scrabble, giving up seven points to keep a strong bingo stem may be easier to justify because the future payoff includes 50 bonus points. In Words With Friends, the same leave still has value, but the smaller bonus makes immediate premium-square scoring relatively more competitive.

This does **not** mean “always chase bingos in Scrabble” or “ignore bingos in WWF.” It means the break-even point changes. Compare:

1. points scored now;
2. quality of the remaining tiles;
3. probability that the board stays open enough to use the leave; and
4. risk created by the lane you open.

If you are ahead on a tight board, closing access may be worth more than preserving a beautiful stem. If you are behind, opening the board and keeping a bingo-friendly leave may be the only route to a comeback.

## Dictionary differences are gameplay differences

Scrabble does not have one universal word list. North American competitive play, international play, household editions, and apps can use different lexicons. Words With Friends validates plays against its own app dictionary, and the accepted vocabulary can vary by language or game implementation.

The MonkeyTactics tools therefore let you search:

- **ENABLE**, a broad public-domain list positioned for North American word-game searches;
- **SOWPODS**, an international English word-game list; or
- **Both**, a deduplicated union for wider word discovery.

Those choices are useful for study, but neither solver is an official Scrabble or Words With Friends dictionary. “Both” is especially unsuitable as proof of legality because it intentionally expands the result set. Before relying on an unfamiliar word, verify it in the game or authority you are actually using.

This matters strategically because hooks and short words are lexicon-dependent. If a two-letter escape or an `S` hook exists in one list but not another, the board can be safer—or more dangerous—than it first appears.

## Strategy differences that matter most

### 1. Value premium access differently

In Scrabble, a modest move that keeps a strong leave can outperform a larger play because the next bingo is worth an extra 50. In WWF, a short play that lands `J`, `V`, or another boosted-value letter on a premium may deserve more weight.

Do not compare word length. Compare the complete turn: all words formed, all active multipliers, and the resulting leave.

### 2. Relearn your high-value letters

Scrabble players instinctively see `H` and `Y` as four-point tiles. Both are worth only three in Words With Friends. Conversely, WWF raises `J` to 10, `V` to 5, and several ordinary consonants to two, three, or four points.

That changes premium-square targeting. A `J` on a triple-letter square begins at 30 points in WWF and 24 in Scrabble. A familiar Scrabble ranking can therefore hide the best WWF play.

### 3. Track the correct bag

Tile tracking depends on distribution as well as value. Scrabble's 100-tile pool and Words With Friends' 104-tile pool produce different expectations about what remains unseen. Do not bring a memorized Scrabble bag count into a WWF endgame.

In either game, tracking becomes more valuable as the bag empties. It tells you whether keeping a `U` for the unseen `Q` is sensible, whether an `S` is likely to remain, and what awkward tiles you might stick your opponent with.

### 4. Treat asynchronous play as analysis time

Classic Scrabble is often played face to face or on a clock. Words With Friends commonly gives each player much longer between moves. Use that time to scan systematically:

1. list every available word and letter premium;
2. inspect parallel plays and two-letter connections;
3. calculate the best immediate scores;
4. compare the leaves;
5. check the strongest reply each candidate allows; and
6. verify unfamiliar words inside the app before committing.

The extra time should improve board vision, not merely produce a longer word.

### 5. Adjust endgame urgency

In both games, unplayed tiles can hurt when the game ends. High-value tiles become liabilities when the board closes. Because the values differ, the danger differs too: a stranded `V` costs five in WWF instead of four, while an `H` or `Y` costs three instead of four.

When few tiles remain, stop optimizing for a theoretical future rack. Find a reliable exit for expensive or inflexible tiles, count the score swing from going out, and avoid leaving your opponent a final premium lane.

## A practical two-game decision rule

When moving from one game to the other, reset three things in your head:

1. **Reset the score table.** Use the correct solver so WWF values do not get mistaken for Scrabble values.
2. **Reset the board map.** Scan the actual premium layout instead of relying on muscle memory.
3. **Reset the payoff horizon.** Scrabble's 50-point bingo makes leave-building more valuable; WWF's 35-point bonus and different premium geometry increase the relative appeal of immediate board scoring.

The best players in either game do the same fundamental work: find legal candidates, calculate the whole board score, protect or improve the rack, and anticipate the reply. Scrabble and Words With Friends simply assign different prices to those decisions.
