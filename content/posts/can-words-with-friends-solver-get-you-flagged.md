If you search for a “safe way to use a Words With Friends solver,” you will find plenty of confident advice about waiting a certain number of seconds, avoiding the top result, or deliberately making mistakes. None of that comes with a guarantee. More importantly, disguising outside help does not turn it into fair play.

The reliable answer is simpler:

> Use a solver for practice, post-game review, and games in which every player has agreed that word finders are allowed. Do not use one to secretly choose moves in a normal competitive game.

That distinction protects more than an account. It keeps the game interesting for the person on the other side of the board, and it turns the solver into a tool that actually improves your skill.

## Can Words With Friends detect solver use?

Zynga does not publish a checklist of Words With Friends cheat-detection signals, scoring thresholds, move-time limits, or “safe” solver behavior. That means claims such as “wait 20 seconds” or “never choose the best word” should not be treated as facts.

Zynga's public [Community Rules](https://www.zynga.com/legal/community-rules) take a broader approach. They prohibit cheats, automation software, bots, unauthorized connections, automated use of the services, and attempts to circumvent game rules or security. Zynga's security guidance also says that identified cheating or Terms of Service violations may lead to action, including suspension or a ban.

Those policies do not create a published exception for a solver that is used slowly, infrequently, or from another device. A local tool may be more private than a server-based one, but privacy is not permission.

### What we should not claim

There is no public Zynga source establishing that the game flags a player for:

- making a move within three to five seconds;
- reaching a particular average score;
- playing a specific number of bingos;
- choosing the mathematically highest-scoring move; or
- improving too quickly.

Some of those patterns might sound plausible. Plausibility is not evidence. Detection systems also change, so reverse-engineered advice can become wrong without notice.

If your goal is to avoid being flagged, the safest approach is not to imitate a weaker player. It is to keep outside move generation out of unassisted games.

## Is using a Words With Friends solver cheating?

The answer depends on the rules and expectations of the activity.

Using a word finder after a finished game is study. Running practice racks outside a live match is study. Using one in a casual game where both players have explicitly agreed to allow reference tools is playing under a house rule.

Secretly entering your live rack and board pattern into a solver, then playing a returned word as your own find, gives you outside assistance that your opponent may not have agreed to. In an ordinary head-to-head game, most players would reasonably call that cheating even if no software connects to Zynga, automates a tap, or accesses an account.

A useful test is disclosure: would you be comfortable telling your opponent exactly how you selected the move? If not, save the search for later.

## The best way to use a solver: review the game afterward

Post-game analysis is both the clearest fair-play use and one of the best ways to learn.

After the game ends, reconstruct one or two positions that felt difficult. Enter the rack you held, add a board pattern if needed, and compare the candidates with the move you made. Do not stop at the first higher-scoring word. Ask why you missed it.

Common causes include:

- overlooking a front or back hook;
- keeping too many duplicate letters;
- failing to separate vowels and consonants while scanning the rack;
- missing a familiar prefix or suffix;
- searching only for long words instead of useful short plays; and
- knowing a word but not recognizing its letters in a new order.

Write down one lesson from the position. A small, specific note—“check every open S-hook before committing”—is more likely to change your next game than memorizing a long list of answers.

## Five fair ways to train with a Words With Friends solver

### 1. Run a post-game missed-move review

Choose three turns from a completed game: one low-scoring turn, one rack that felt awkward, and one important board decision. Search each rack and build a short list of plausible alternatives.

Then compare the whole move, not only its base tile score. Consider rack leave, board access, hooks, and what the play would give your opponent. A solver can produce candidates; it cannot fully reconstruct strategy unless you supply and evaluate the position.

### 2. Practice rack recognition away from live games

Give yourself a rack, start a timer, and write down every word you can find. Search only after time expires. This preserves the act of finding words while giving you immediate feedback.

The MonkeyTactics rack controls can group vowels and consonants, expose duplicate tiles, move high-value letters together, or arrange common letter stems. Try the rack manually first, then compare your arrangement with these alternatives.

### 3. Study hooks in small batches

A hook adds a letter to the front or back of a word to create another valid word. Instead of trying to memorize hundreds at once, choose five words from a completed search and inspect their hooks.

Turn them into a quick recall drill the next day. This converts a solver result into durable pattern knowledge rather than a one-time answer.

### 4. Build vocabulary with definitions

An unfamiliar result is not useful knowledge until you can recognize and remember it. Open the local definition, say the word aloud, note its part of speech, and create a short association.

Also check legality against the dictionary used by your game. MonkeyTactics offers Standard ENABLE, an Expanded Wiktionary-derived list, and a combined list. These are useful search resources, but MonkeyTactics is not the official Words With Friends dictionary, and a result in a broad dictionary is not a promise that the app will accept it.

### 5. Compare rack leaves

The highest immediate score is not always the strongest strategic choice. Practice comparing what remains after each candidate play. Balanced, flexible letters may be worth more on the next turn than an awkward cluster of duplicates.

## A fair-play solver checklist

Before opening a word finder, ask:

- Has the live game ended?
- If it has not ended, did every player clearly agree that solvers are allowed?
- Am I using the result to learn a pattern, rather than to pass off a generated move as my own?
- Am I keeping automation and unauthorized account or game connections out of the workflow?
- Will I verify unfamiliar words against the dictionary and rules that apply to my game?

If the first two answers are no, close the solver and return after the match.

## A simple 15-minute study routine

You do not need to analyze an entire game to improve.

1. Pick one difficult rack from a completed match.
2. Spend three minutes finding candidates without assistance.
3. Search the rack and save three interesting results to the Pick List.
4. Check their definitions, hooks, scores, and rack leaves.
5. Write down one reusable pattern.
6. Rebuild the rack the next day and test yourself again before searching.

Repeated recall is the important step. Looking at a surprising word once can feel productive, but retrieving it later is what makes it part of your playable vocabulary.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can Zynga ban you for using a solver?

Zynga says cheats, bots, automation, and other violations of its rules can lead to account action. It does not publish a solver-specific detection formula or a guaranteed list of permitted solver uses. Review the current rules yourself, avoid automation and unauthorized connections, and reserve word-finder searches for post-game study or explicitly agreed games.

### What is the safest way to use a Words With Friends solver?

Use it after the game. Revisit a difficult rack, compare alternatives, study hooks and definitions, and test yourself again later. This avoids hidden outside assistance and produces genuine improvement.

### Can I use a solver during a game if I do not choose the top result?

Choosing the second- or third-ranked result does not change the source of the move. If the other player expects an unassisted game, secretly using a solver remains outside assistance. There is also no published “safe ranking” that prevents account action.

### Does waiting before playing make solver use undetectable?

No public Zynga policy promises that a particular delay is safe, and timing a move to disguise outside help is not a fair-play strategy. Use the tool after the match instead.
